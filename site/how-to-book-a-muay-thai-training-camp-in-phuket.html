How to Book a Muay Thai Camp in Phuket (And Actually Show Up Ready)

So you have decided to do it. You are going to Phuket to train Muay Thai. Good decision.

Now comes the part most people overthink: actually booking it. This guide cuts through the noise and walks you through exactly how to book a Muay Thai camp in Phuket, what to look for, and how to make sure your first session does not wreck you.

Step 1: Decide What You Actually Want From This

Before you book anything, get clear on one thing: what does a good trip look like for you?

There are three types of people who come to Phuket to train:

The fitness traveller. You want to train hard, sweat more than you ever have at home, lose some weight, and come back with a story. You have never done Muay Thai before or you have done a few classes. You want guidance, not a fight contract.

The serious hobbyist. You train back home. You want two sessions a day with real Thai trainers who will actually push your technique, not just let you hit pads for an hour.

The fighter. You have bouts coming up. You need specific preparation, sparring, and a trainer who knows what they are doing at competition level.

Phuket has gyms for all three. Know which one you are before you book. It changes everything from the training intensity to the accommodation, the schedule, and how long you should stay.

Step 2: Pick the Right Part of Phuket

Phuket is not small. Where your gym sits matters.

Kathu sits in the centre of the island. Quieter than Patong, closer than the southern tips, easy access to everything without the chaos of the tourist strip. It is where you come when you want to actually train, not just take Instagram photos of a ring.

Phuket King Muay Thai is based in Kathu, right on the lake. The setting matters: when you are training twice a day and your body is asking questions, having space to breathe, recover, and reset makes a real difference.

Step 3: Look for These Things in a Camp

Not all gyms are equal. When you are comparing options, check for these:

Qualified trainers with fight experience. Ask who the trainers are. At PKMT, Kru Max, Kru Frong, and Kru Pot have all competed at serious levels. You want people who have been in the ring, not just held pads.

Small class sizes. Big camps can run 30 or 40 students per session. That means less time on pads with a trainer and more time waiting. Smaller camps give you actual attention.

Accommodation on site or nearby. Commuting to training twice a day is exhausting. Staying at or near the gym keeps your recovery time protected and your schedule clean.

Recognition and ratings. Phuket King Muay Thai is recognised and rated 5 stars by the Sports Authority of Thailand. That is not a marketing badge. It is an official government certification that the training, safety, and standards meet a serious benchmark.

A real environment. PKMT has a full ring, heavy bags, air-conditioned bungalows, a lakeside setting, an ice bath, a herbal sauna, and a cafe on site. This is a complete training environment, not just a room with bags in it.

Step 4: Choose Your Package and Length of Stay

One of the most common questions we get: how long should I stay?

Here is a straightforward answer:

One week is the minimum to start feeling the rhythm of training and see a real change. Our Zero Decision Week is built for exactly this: seven nights all-inclusive, two sessions a day, no logistics to manage, just train. It was designed for people who are burned out and need a full reset, not a holiday.

Two weeks is where most people find their groove. Your body adapts, your technique improves noticeably, and you start actually looking forward to morning sessions.

One month or more is where real transformation happens. Longer-term guests are welcome and the camp has the infrastructure to support extended stays.

Step 6: Prepare Before You Arrive

You do not need to be fit to start training Muay Thai. But a few things make your first week easier:

Get some cardio in your legs. Even 20 minutes of running a few times a week before you fly helps. Training twice a day is hard on your cardiovascular system early on.

Bring the right gear. While most gyms do lend out gloves, hand wraps and shinguards, it is worth brining your own if you have them already, or purchasing some on your arrival. Borrowing is a short term fix, but having your own is just better, more hygienic, and well, it's yours.

Come with an open mind about rest. Recovery is part of training. The herbal sauna, ice bath, and breath-work space at PKMT are optional extras. But should if you ae able be utilized as part of your training program. They are part of what makes the training sustainable.

Bring a DTV visa if you are planning an extended stay. Phuket King also offers Destination Thailand Visa documentation services if you need support with that. This helps massively if you plan to train for a while, and regular. PKMT also offers a 3 month Muay Thai education visa, consider this also.

What Happens on Day One

The first session is not designed to destroy you. It is designed to show you where you are and what the training looks like. By the end of day three, you will have more energy than you expected. By the end of the week, you will not want to leave.

That is not a sales line. That is what the reviews say, consistently, from people who came from the UK, USA, Australia, Germany, Dubai, and everywhere in between.
