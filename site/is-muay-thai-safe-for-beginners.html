Is Muay Thai Safe for Beginners? An Honest Answer

We get asked this a lot, usually a little nervously: "I have never done Muay Thai before. Is that okay?"

Yes. Absolutely.

Muay Thai has fighting in its nature, but for a beginner it is one of the most relaxed sports you can start, and it is kind to your body. A good gym, with a good philosophy, will be genuinely happy to have you and will love helping you begin your Muay Thai journey.

Fighters are some of the gentlest people you will meet

It surprises a lot of people, but some of the gentlest, kindest people you will ever meet are Thai Muay Thai fighters. They are calm, humble and patient, and most of them love teaching beginners.

What really decides how a gym feels is its culture. The trainers and the members make the atmosphere. So choose a gym that:

  • is happy to have beginners and welcomes them,
  • has a proper programme for beginners,
  • has a good history of teaching people from day one.

So, is it dangerous?

No. That could not be further from the truth for someone starting out.

Like any sport, you need to listen to your body and manage your expectations. But a beginner class is pad work, bag work, technique and fitness. Nobody throws you into a fight.

If anything, Muay Thai is the opposite of dangerous. It builds confidence and self esteem, and people who train tend to be calmer and less likely to be aggressive outside the gym, not more.

What a beginner class really involves

  • A warm up and stretching.
  • Learning the stance, guard and footwork.
  • The basic strikes: jab, cross, round kick and teep.
  • Rounds on the pads with a coach.
  • Bag work and conditioning.

What about sparring?

Sparring is optional. Many people train for years and never spar hard or fight. Read no, you do not have to fight.

If you do want to spar:

  • Choose a gym with a good sparring culture, one that does not like aggressive sparring.
  • Make sure you are matched fairly with someone at your level who controls their power.
  • If someone hits you hard, by accident or on purpose, just walk away. Tell a coach, and do not spar with that person again.

How to start safely

  • Start slowly. One class a day for the first week is plenty.
  • Learn technique first. Good technique protects your joints. That is why we teach technique first at Phuket King.
  • Drink water and electrolytes. Phuket is hot and you will sweat a lot.
  • Rest and recover. Sleep, eat well, and use the ice bath, herbal steam room and sauna.
  • Tell your coach. If something hurts, say so, and they will adapt the session.
  • Train within your level. There is no rush.

The bottom line

Train at a welcoming gym, stay within your level and listen to your body, and Muay Thai is a safe, friendly sport to start, at any age and any fitness level.
