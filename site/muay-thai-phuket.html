Muay Thai in Phuket

Phuket is one of the best places in the world to learn Muay Thai. There are gyms all over the island, for complete beginners and for full time fighters. This is our local guide: where to train, what it costs, when to come and what a week of training looks like.

Why train Muay Thai in Phuket?

Muay Thai is Thailand's national sport, and in Phuket it is part of everyday life. You train with Thai coaches who grew up in the sport, alongside people from all over the world, in a place where you can recover on the beach or by a lake between sessions.

  • Real Thai coaching. Most coaches have fought for years and teach the way they were taught.
  • Every level is welcome. Beginners, fitness training, weight loss and fight camps all happen in the same gyms.
  • Train as much as you like. Most gyms run two or three group classes a day, six days a week, plus private lessons.
  • Easy to fit into a trip. Drop in for one class, train for a week, or stay for months.

Where to train: the main areas

Where you train shapes your whole trip, so pick the area first, then the gym.

When to come

  • November to April is the dry season: sunny, a little less humid, and the busiest time in the gyms.
  • May to October is the rainy season: short heavy showers, quieter gyms, and cheaper rooms.

Gyms train all year round. It is hot every month, so drink more water than you think you need and take the first few days easy.

What a training week looks like

A typical group class is about two hours: a run or skipping to warm up, stretching and shadow boxing, technique, pad rounds with a coach, bag work, clinch or partner drills, then conditioning. At Phuket King each day has its own technique focus, so over a week you work every weapon.

  • Morning class: Monday to Saturday, 8:00
  • Afternoon class: Monday to Saturday, 16:30
  • Evening class: Monday to Friday, 18:30
  • Sunday: no group classes. Open 10:00 to 20:00 for private classes, the Fitness Zone and the recovery spa

How to choose a gym

  • Watch a class or try one. Look at how the coaches treat beginners, not just the fighters.
  • Ask about class size and how many coaches are on the floor.
  • Check the timetable fits the way you want to train.
  • Look at recovery. An ice bath, sauna and somewhere to rest make a big difference over a week or more.
  • Read recent reviews on Google.

Muay Thai in Phuket: common questions

Can complete beginners train Muay Thai in Phuket?

Yes. Most gyms in Phuket teach beginners every day in the same classes as experienced students, and the coaches split the room by level. You do not need to be fit, and sparring is never forced on beginners.

How much does Muay Thai cost in Phuket?

It depends on the gym and how long you train. At Phuket King a single group class is 400 THB, a week of unlimited classes is 2,500 THB, a month is 7,000 THB and a private lesson is 900 THB.

Which area of Phuket is best for Muay Thai?

Chalong (Soi Ta-iad) is best if you only want hard fight training. Kathu suits people who want great technique, a calmer and more local area, and to be 10 minutes from Patong and Phuket Town.

Do I need to book a group class?

At Phuket King, no. Come ten minutes early, check in at the desk and train. Private lessons are booked ahead in the app or on WhatsApp.

Are gyms open on Sundays?

Many gyms rest on Sunday. Phuket King has no group classes on Sunday, but is open 10:00 to 20:00 for private classes, the Fitness Zone and the recovery spa.

What should I bring?

Shorts, a t-shirt or vest, a big water bottle and a small towel. Gloves can be borrowed, and hand wraps and gear are sold at the gym.
